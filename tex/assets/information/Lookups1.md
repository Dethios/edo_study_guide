**Interaction with DoD Acquisitions and Control:**

CAPE's power is one of influence, not command. It has **no direct decision authority or line responsibility** over acquisition programs and cannot direct a Program Manager to take a specific action []. However, its direct reporting line to the Secretary of Defense means its analyses heavily influence major acquisition decisions []. A negative assessment from CAPE can lead to a program being restructured, delayed, or canceled by the Milestone Decision Authority (MDA) [].

CAPE's interaction is most formally defined in its oversight of ICEs and AoAs.

The core execution limit is that an agency **cannot obligate or spend more money than has been appropriated by Congress and apportioned by OMB** []. It is illegal to spend in advance of or in excess of an appropriation or apportionment []. The apportionment process is designed to prevent agencies from spending their entire budget early in the fiscal year and to promote the economical use of government funds [[35]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFsIGNJhgR0LGxtIAaU2eAI5STSlGQjR0a8BM7X9S6ue98fEYHJKxZ4DeBGMLeimXn5h_SJvLJCD2epk2Kfa34zYN8tj7Y6awIyfxJyo8mEECjX8Xo6VwOhshRrX5yIGIqNezrA_76ot9zUw0bmERC0PZe3PPX4rHVu-YmG7Q1J2JYdHls2wPNm4Oi8TnLPs6qSMyYfXQ==)[[25]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQE1DzxHWjmJD69jU2ZpXwjzsFsOAyvj1hkZl7s9wvxSzz-oeaGmkmHkXC2cqcyGu5YNsmGKZ-56QOfZD5ZbS9dordbbP-GDDV2kwkMaRexRK9j7EeM0LUXa8d-DVkkxYuUj-_OomV6dWFLoh95e5UQHvPeJamJHwRS8-eb-IENtx82AUY8noXuLZwovpgLZOnFNJqyjj2R-tswenVMrH-mJUu7_k4Q5pt-oqyOLT5BPH1J0K2b9q5FV0e9hryyfOZ1vO1h8CDA-FnryPaXqbluc) . #### **3. How does a Program Manager understand if their execution/expenditure is tracking expectations?**

A Program Manager (PM) in the DoD primarily uses **Earned Value Management (EVM)** to understand if their program's execution and expenditures are tracking with expectations []. EVM is a powerful program management tool that integrates the technical scope of work with the schedule and cost elements []. The methodology is mandated for all major DoD contracts and is based on the 32 guidelines in the **EIA-748 standard** [].

The core of EVM involves comparing planned work to completed work to determine if cost and schedule performance are on track []. This is done by measuring three key metrics, typically on a monthly basis:

1.  **Budgeted Cost for Work Scheduled (BCWS):** This is the time-phased budget plan []. It represents the value of the work planned to be accomplished as of a specific date []. It is also known as the Performance Measurement Baseline (PMB) [].
2.  **Budgeted Cost for Work Performed (BCWP):** This is the "earned value." It is the value of the work that has actually been completed as of that same date [].
3.  **Actual Cost of Work Performed (ACWP):** This is the cost actually incurred to accomplish the work that has been completed []. This data comes from the contractor's accounting system [].

From these three metrics, a PM can determine their performance against expectations by calculating two critical variances:

*   **Schedule Variance (SV):**
    *   **Formula:** SV = BCWP - BCWS [].
    *   **Interpretation:** This variance measures whether the program is ahead of or behind schedule in dollar terms [].
        *   A **positive SV** is favorable (more work has been done than was planned) [].
        *   A **negative SV** is unfavorable (less work has been done than was planned), indicating the program is behind schedule [].

*   **Cost Variance (CV):**
    *   **Formula:** CV = BCWP - ACWP [].
    *   **Interpretation:** This variance measures whether the program is under or over budget for the work performed [].
        *   A **positive CV** is favorable (the work completed cost less than planned) [].
        *   A **negative CV** is unfavorable (the work completed cost more than planned), indicating a cost overrun [].

By analyzing these variances, a PM can gain immediate insight:

*   If SV is negative, the program is behind schedule [].
*   If CV is negative, the program is over budget [].
*   If both are negative, the program is both behind schedule and over budget, a situation requiring immediate management attention [].

Furthermore, PMs use EVM data to forecast future performance, most notably the **Estimate at Completion (EAC)**, which projects the total cost of the program based on the performance to date []. This allows the PM to understand not just their current status but also their likely future status, enabling proactive management and reporting to stakeholders [].

#### **5. Explain the congressional marks and reclama process, including how congress issues a mark against a program, how a program office reverses the mark, and the reclama process.**

The concept of "marks" is central to the congressional budget process, while the term "reclama" is specific to the executive branch's internal budget formulation []. They are related but distinct processes [].

**Congressional Marks**

A "mark" is a term for any change—an addition, cut, or policy provision—that a congressional committee makes to the President's Budget request during the legislative process []. These marks occur primarily within the annual **National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA)** and the **Defense Appropriations Act** [].

*   **How Congress Issues a Mark:**
    1.  **President's Budget Submission:** The process begins when the President submits the annual budget request to Congress, detailing the funding desired for every DoD program [].
    2.  **Committee Hearings:** The House Armed Services Committee (HASC) and Senate Armed Services Committee (SASC), along with their appropriations counterparts, hold hearings to question DoD officials about the budget request [].
    3.  **Subcommittee and Full Committee Markup:** This is where the "marking" happens []. Each subcommittee drafts its version of the bill, making changes (marks) to the President's request []. They might add funding, cut funding, or add restrictive language []. These marks are then rolled up and further amended at the full committee level [].
    4.  **Floor Action and Conference:** Each chamber passes its own version of the bill []. The differences are then resolved in a conference committee, which produces the final bill to be voted on and sent to the President [].

**How a Program Office "Reverses" a Mark**

A program office cannot unilaterally "reverse" a congressional mark; direct communication with Congress is prohibited [[1]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQF9k7MkDCGVuBhpZd0ajVgnmat9MF8vmxxRYoWoWj9LxMQFuFPAzCJ5jCGjbQRiN1ck99qjzbIbphgWP9sx0groooZ6wiGUIS0sDHxWtRX2ujK1HC_ipA7hcegaLJ4uvC_Nx6DrhJUug5UwOI0Q4pxMiBqcD5rMP60pr8Oh9Ir8Z4oI9PKtQQCW5xcic-bgwPf5)[[21]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQEwD7J5l3nyFtv6x4Tpv1M5_vGvVXn9odap2D72cFLwJqc0MNhH_pB0b54yIdTpzW7_5rdz3f20DU5HyIqaN1KsTQUA-nq--cuBNdYzyyTuQ8saMPTVL0VMZZjioDBJopbndivSBMGZAgQVLkxP1X4QZjS6yogbtwUrEL79I80dRc5bT4PgCyzYFurP40qB1N3QmazqgykqGpPLuVlWEuueqpwrqUKN1PjtSBMUrWYiWcLYZ295-7NOCkY=) . Instead, it must engage in a complex and politically sensitive process of advocacy through official channels to persuade Congress to change the mark before it becomes law [[28]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFVur4B_WVqU8-mCTCYHBeQUUJO2wwXQEYOD0DYrLPFM4FnR63ejNVSSGgVo0EFMAgo1fHRa4AhOAkp_Pd3QKXHRdUnCLKQqZz5PIWb8u3j5B-xf0swP7p1t6_3_w0h3Ww5qJHAFDZvtnWAVA9tp22tO8wifnHKA95EKNwjr-OqQTjPgO-tYCxGpXBjkCDawYaHaqN2JTi7w0QjpqZHxl8xSaKzOhg3ldZ4wMDd8C4xMKGjYLArcUPSxLHGFn_yh4jvyD6Rea2t-S9WYt9DrQQqxx8E9glGUFEYahPJHyeDy8Ss9ii_AOxB) . This entire process is orchestrated by the service's **Office of Legislative Affairs (OLA)**, such as the Army's Office of the Chief Legislative Liaison (OCLL) [[29]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQEgAzE6pq1LjvPObkhbbJZx4JdcpOxdPXE0aKjvYhwI-TadWLrCB9Hu_VADSPKsjhYTuT15vt-alJJLet5ycrXBYnd8qt6Gq-PHn8cHp6xjtyoupsWdAY0-MLOjFS-EcDM5fsEUA7KHJljmBM812FA4) .

The process involves the following steps:

1.  **Develop a Robust Justification:** The program office must create a compelling, evidence-based argument explaining why the mark is detrimental . This justification must be vetted and approved through the internal chain of command (PEO, service acquisition executive) and coordinated with offices like the OSD Comptroller . The justification should include:
    *   A clear analysis of the impact on the program's cost, schedule, and performance .
    *   A description of the operational consequences for the warfighter and any increased risk .
    *   A rebuttal to the committee's stated concerns, explaining why they may be based on incomplete information .
    *   Any new data or changes in circumstances that warrant reconsideration .
2.  **Strategic Engagement with the Office of Legislative Affairs (OLA):** With leadership backing, the program office engages its service's OLA [[28]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFVur4B_WVqU8-mCTCYHBeQUUJO2wwXQEYOD0DYrLPFM4FnR63ejNVSSGgVo0EFMAgo1fHRa4AhOAkp_Pd3QKXHRdUnCLKQqZz5PIWb8u3j5B-xf0swP7p1t6_3_w0h3Ww5qJHAFDZvtnWAVA9tp22tO8wifnHKA95EKNwjr-OqQTjPgO-tYCxGpXBjkCDawYaHaqN2JTi7w0QjpqZHxl8xSaKzOhg3ldZ4wMDd8C4xMKGjYLArcUPSxLHGFn_yh4jvyD6Rea2t-S9WYt9DrQQqxx8E9glGUFEYahPJHyeDy8Ss9ii_AOxB) . 3.  **Communication with Congressional Staff:** The OLA facilitates all communications with the professional staff of the key defense committees [[21]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQEwD7J5l3nyFtv6x4Tpv1M5_vGvVXn9odap2D72cFLwJqc0MNhH_pB0b54yIdTpzW7_5rdz3f20DU5HyIqaN1KsTQUA-nq--cuBNdYzyyTuQ8saMPTVL0VMZZjioDBJopbndivSBMGZAgQVLkxP1X4QZjS6yogbtwUrEL79I80dRc5bT4PgCyzYFurP40qB1N3QmazqgykqGpPLuVlWEuueqpwrqUKN1PjtSBMUrWYiWcLYZ295-7NOCkY=)[[29]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQEgAzE6pq1LjvPObkhbbJZx4JdcpOxdPXE0aKjvYhwI-TadWLrCB9Hu_VADSPKsjhYTuT15vt-alJJLet5ycrXBYnd8qt6Gq-PHn8cHp6xjtyoupsWdAY0-MLOjFS-EcDM5fsEUA7KHJljmBM812FA4) . This is done through several avenues:
    *   **Formal Responses:** Submitting official letters, issue papers, or other documentation presenting the DoD's position .
    *   **Informal Briefings:** Program office subject matter experts, accompanied by OLA staff, conduct briefings for committee staff to explain the negative repercussions of the mark . This is often the most effective method .
    *   **Senior Leader Engagement:** For high-priority issues, senior DoD leaders may engage directly with members of Congress [[28]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFVur4B_WVqU8-mCTCYHBeQUUJO2wwXQEYOD0DYrLPFM4FnR63ejNVSSGgVo0EFMAgo1fHRa4AhOAkp_Pd3QKXHRdUnCLKQqZz5PIWb8u3j5B-xf0swP7p1t6_3_w0h3Ww5qJHAFDZvtnWAVA9tp22tO8wifnHKA95EKNwjr-OqQTjPgO-tYCxGpXBjkCDawYaHaqN2JTi7w0QjpqZHxl8xSaKzOhg3ldZ4wMDd8C4xMKGjYLArcUPSxLHGFn_yh4jvyD6Rea2t-S9WYt9DrQQqxx8E9glGUFEYahPJHyeDy8Ss9ii_AOxB) . This can include so-called **"Heartburn" letters** from the Secretary of Defense expressing strong opposition to a mark [[1]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQF9k7MkDCGVuBhpZd0ajVgnmat9MF8vmxxRYoWoWj9LxMQFuFPAzCJ5jCGjbQRiN1ck99qjzbIbphgWP9sx0groooZ6wiGUIS0sDHxWtRX2ujK1HC_ipA7hcegaLJ4uvC_Nx6DrhJUug5UwOI0Q4pxMiBqcD5rMP60pr8Oh9Ir8Z4oI9PKtQQCW5xcic-bgwPf5) .
4.  **Leveraging the Legislative Process:** The goal is to persuade Congress to reverse the mark during subsequent stages of the legislative process . The best opportunities are:

**The "Reclama" Process (Executive Branch Context)**

The term **"reclama"** refers to a formal appeal process *within the executive branch* during the formulation of the President's Budget [[1]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQF9k7MkDCGVuBhpZd0ajVgnmat9MF8vmxxRYoWoWj9LxMQFuFPAzCJ5jCGjbQRiN1ck99qjzbIbphgWP9sx0groooZ6wiGUIS0sDHxWtRX2ujK1HC_ipA7hcegaLJ4uvC_Nx6DrhJUug5UwOI0Q4pxMiBqcD5rMP60pr8Oh9Ir8Z4oI9PKtQQCW5xcic-bgwPf5) . It is the mechanism used to challenge internal budget decisions before they are finalized and sent to Congress [].

*   **How it Works:**
    1.  During the Navy's internal POM development, a resource sponsor (e.g., N96) submits its budget proposal (the SPP) [[22]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQF_VjlXbHIOVhyCcPgijdggamdRNDqC9piXGpXZWom3yhf20KNF5axOYafY8DteQdM07PFDFr5k_MW-aid43mYN-f1Xf06ul1W1E1F6CrRKS67wsH3etnz4cNcE28DUXiHVs3W9P36LEe9SuK9UrHrsF4ftD9aaq4_C1FMLDWju9X1hGKKnxpfdWMbwUFE=)[[26]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFD3BY8S7GzyiBpyU7jRrOVYAb3mjBqA9Mi9mST3s4kFKgQt0uM2W2B1L7O0ulrncJbvrMwSA9CHxlMoGeNY-4597yDzCvwzYMWjLjLjanE6LptCj7VhlIoNa4iTU3dsB2tEfMFb30ab3fgxAY9ny7OmdBL9FaEGgmc1ODUIPjlhZdozcNhtzhc66JFXfMf9qbOf5Y=) .
    2.  During the integration phase, a higher authority (e.g., OPNAV N80, or later, OSD CAPE) may decide to cut the program's proposed budget [[40]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQH1EvCyGcsqww31g1PY-J8OOEtjtgxAWwmknKOmkLjnCZeNHDLT4LfGIysdi_v4ewdKzIvmBefvZT_Vkx-cXoFJ8hI1UoRpEplmWZ_RQubRhRZ8zgoYq6ESEH-VppFbor6kL6PEaRI2qi7nbvnKDkcuLhujD9Zq6rOl-FhSaKiDQ9Jkuolbr-r5Y8egjaJ4RcGwUeHb_1wdtOA82vPy9hEGYJ5C_iRAPwrm2cxJY5lMPvNCmmYql9i_GMY=)[[26]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQFD3BY8S7GzyiBpyU7jRrOVYAb3mjBqA9Mi9mST3s4kFKgQt0uM2W2B1L7O0ulrncJbvrMwSA9CHxlMoGeNY-4597yDzCvwzYMWjLjLjanE6LptCj7VhlIoNa4iTU3dsB2tEfMFb30ab3fgxAY9ny7OmdBL9FaEGgmc1ODUIPjlhZdozcNhtzhc66JFXfMf9qbOf5Y=) .

The final decision from the Secretary of Defense is documented in a **Resource Management Decision (RMD)**, which directs the final changes to the service's POM submission [[39]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQH9e7f27cEBOuqDd4x-6jb0GcvbZilGkdvRIg8etv6kdZmyRPlF38xS6oNouhdPwVjFHlLt_6WBWIrWlP0qhvlRBzdlr3Iux78x2K_DVfLWL-A6GvZM_BS1koPodRqxQ1lHEOgIBA2PM9CDC5G12VwtdmS1Xr_V204Uyjs-5_nRK6ESh7udwZgR7yc5euOW30B_)[[13]](https://vertexaisearch.cloud.google.com/grounding-api-redirect/AUZIYQE5T6GpAZHuubsxDl8-ixBkeglgbSEt3yRA1f2rW4hVH8W9nB7PhnqfF3Qs0JTlImFZi2mFe2LotBfCm5_JRdKb4-gz3yJNZLWjNkwIA9cWrpIeBzvTFSX8xU7DmIkAUa5VGpp3SMak4vPrHc-D5SVl8zerDw==) .

#### **When a change of command occurs, how long does the new OIC have to make changes before unions consider old actions permanent?**

That's an excellent question that gets to the heart of a commander's authority and the complexities of labor-management relations, particularly concerning civilian personnel.

There is no specific, legally defined "grace period" or time limit (e.g., 30, 60, or 90 days) for a new Officer in Charge (OIC) to implement changes before a previous way of doing things becomes permanent.

The issue is governed by the concept of **"Past Practice"** in federal labor law, which is administered by the Federal Labor Relations Authority (FLRA).

### **Understanding "Past Practice"**

A "past practice" is a consistent, long-standing way of doing things that is **known and accepted** by both management (including previous OICs) and employees. For a practice to become legally binding, it generally must be:

1.  **Clear and Consistent:** The practice has been applied consistently over a period of time.
2.  **Long-standing:** It has existed for a reasonably long duration. There is no magic number of months or years; the determination is made on a case-by-case basis.
3.  **Mutually Accepted:** Both management and employees have knowingly acquiesced to the practice.

If these conditions are met, the practice can become a **binding condition of employment**, even if it is not written down in any regulation or collective bargaining agreement.

### **How This Affects a New OIC**

A new OIC absolutely has the authority to change command procedures. The critical issue is *how* those changes are made.

*   **The Risk of Waiting:** The longer a new OIC waits to change a procedure they inherited, the stronger the argument becomes that they have accepted it, solidifying it as a binding "past practice."
*   **The Obligation to Bargain:** Once a procedure is established as a past practice, a commander cannot typically change it unilaterally. Doing so without providing the union with notice and an opportunity to bargain over the change (or the "impact and implementation" of the change) can be considered an **Unfair Labor Practice (ULP)**. This could lead to a formal complaint filed against the command.
*   **Initial Assessment Period:** While there is no formal "grace period" to make unilateral changes, there is a recognized window for a new commander to get oriented. A **Change of Command Command Climate Assessment (CCA)** must occur within **90 days** after assuming command. This period is intended for the new OIC to review trends and assess the command climate before implementing their own vision. This 90-day assessment window is the closest thing to the "initial change" period you asked about, but it does not grant the OIC the right to ignore established past practices during that time.

### **Conclusion**

In short, there is no set time limit. An OIC can implement changes on their first day. However, if a procedure is already a well-established "past practice," the OIC has an obligation to bargain with the civilian employees' union representative *before* making a change.

Waiting to make a change doesn't create this obligation, it simply strengthens the evidence that a binding past practice already exists. Therefore, it is wisest for a new OIC to identify procedures they wish to change early and, if a past practice exists, to engage the union through the proper channels rather than assuming they have a "grace period" to act alone.
