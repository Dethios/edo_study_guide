\section{NAVWAR Enterprise}

\subsection{Team NAVWAR overview}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Organization.}] The \ac{navwar} enterprise consists of the \ac{navwar} systems command (Echelon~II), three affiliated \acp{peo} (\ac{peoc4i}, \ac{peodigital}, \ac{peomlb}), and three Echelon~III activities: \ac{niwclant}, \ac{niwcpac}, and \ac{nsfa}~\autocite{edo-2-1-3-navwar-enterprise-2024}.
  \item[\textbf{Current PAE alignment.}] Current public structure also shows DRPM Project Overmatch and the \ac{don}'s PAE Mission Systems, publicly established on 11~May~2026. PAE Mission Systems pulls together PEO C4I, PEO Digital, PEO IWS, PEO MLB, DRPM Overmatch, DRPM LRNFO, DRPM MILE, Minotaur from PMA-290, and mission-system elements of NAVWAR, NAVSEA, NAVAIR, and Marine Corps Systems Command~\autocite{DON-PAE-Expansion-May2026,DON-PAE-Mission-Systems-2026}. Treat this as current acquisition-governance realignment while official NAVWAR pages continue to present the core NAVWAR enterprise and PEO pages unevenly.
  \item[\textbf{Team NAVWAR.}] More than 11{,}000 employees support fleet concentration areas and deployed operations worldwide~\autocite{edo-2-1-3-navwar-enterprise-2024}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Mission}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Mission statement.}] Identify, develop, deliver, and sustain information warfighting capabilities and services that enable naval, joint, coalition, and other national missions from seabed to space, while providing research and development, systems engineering, test and evaluation, technical, in-service, and support services to \acp{peo} across program life cycles~\autocite{edo-2-1-3-navwar-enterprise-2024}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Echelon III activities and core competencies}
\subsubsection{NIWC Pacific}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Mission.}] Conduct research, development, prototyping, engineering, test and evaluation, installation, and sustainment of integrated \ac{c4isr}, cyber, and space systems across all warfighting domains~\autocite{edo-2-1-3-navwar-enterprise-2024}.
  \item[\textbf{Technology areas.}] Cyber operations; command and control communications and networks; production, installation, and in-service engineering; intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance and information operations; science and technology; marine mammal programs; autonomous unmanned systems~\autocite{edo-2-1-3-navwar-enterprise-2024}.

\subsubsection{PMA/PMW 101: Multifunctional Information Distribution Systems}
\label{ssubsec:pma-pmw101}

\textbf{Program:} Multifunctional Information Distribution Systems (MIDS)

PMA/PMW~101 develops, produces, and sustains tactical data-link products and waveforms, including Link~16 and Tactical Targeting Network Technology, to provide interoperable tactical data exchange across air, surface, shore, coalition, and international users. The current public tear sheet lists the office at Naval Base Point Loma, Harbor Drive Annex, 33050 Nixie Way, Building~17A, Suite~416, San Diego~\autocite{PEOC4I-PMA-PMW101-tearsheet-2025}.

\textbf{Board cue:} PMA/PMW~101 is the MIDS/Link~16 office. It is adjacent to C4I recall, but do not confuse it with PMW~160: MIDS/Link~16 is the tactical data-link/waveform lane, while CANES/ADNS are the afloat network and routing lanes. Do not normalize every cross-\ac{navwar}/\ac{navair} office to the same slash convention. Current PEO C4I and \ac{navair} sources style the MIDS office as PMA/PMW~101 or PMA/PMW-101. Use that exact form for MIDS/Link~16\autocite{PEOC4I-PMA-PMW101-tearsheet-2025,NAVAIR-PMA-PMW101}.

\subsubsection{PMW 120: Battlespace Awareness and Information Operations}
\label{ssubsec:pmw120}

\textbf{Program:} \ac{dcgsn}

\textbf{Status cue:} Current public status is Software Acquisition Pathway; legacy coursebook status was Increment~1 (ACAT IAC) and Increment~2 (ACAT IAM)~\autocite{PEOC4I-PMW120-tearsheet-2026}.

PMW 120 focuses on creating and breaking ``kill webs'' for the warfighter by delivering integrated intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance (ISR), targeting, and electronic warfare capabilities. \ac{dcgsn} is the Navy's flagship intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance, and targeting support capability and is part of the broader DoD DCGS ecosystem~\autocite{PEOC4I-PMW120-tearsheet-2026}.

\ac{dcgsn} is designed to provide multiservice integration of ISR and targeting capabilities. The legacy board cue is that \ac{dcgsn} consolidated intelligence analytical tools into an integrated computing environment and then evolved toward more agile software delivery. For current-status answers, call it \ac{dcgsn} Family of Systems under PMW~120 and state the Software Acquisition Pathway unless a current source supports a different designation. Do not expand the acronym as ``Digital Consolidated'' or ``Maritime''; the board-safe expansion is \emph{Distributed Common Ground System-Navy}.

\textbf{Strategic Insights:} The evolution of \ac{dcgsn} highlights a broader trend within NAVWAR ACAT I programs: the migration from hardware-heavy, monolithic deployments to agile, cloud-based software architectures. PMW 120's strategy involves transitioning from sustaining isolated systems to deploying afloat and ashore cloud environments. However, this transition introduces significant challenges in software growth, with instances of up to 30-percent increases in \ac{sloc} growth, requiring careful cost estimation and rigorous continuous testing.

\subsubsection{PMW 150: Naval Command and Control Systems}
\label{ssubsec:pmw150}

PMW 150 is the Navy's preeminent provider of operational and tactical command and control solutions. Current public materials keep GCCS-M as an ACAT IAC program; NTCSS remains useful legacy coursebook context, but it is not confirmed on the current PMW~150 top-program sheet~\autocite{PEOC4I-PMW150-tearsheet-2026}.

\textbf{Program 1: Global Command and Control System-Maritime (GCCS-M)}

\textbf{ACAT Status:} ACAT IAC~\autocite{PEOC4I-PMW150-tearsheet-2026}

GCCS-M is the Navy's primary \ac{c2} program of record and the maritime iteration of the DoD's Joint \ac{c2} Family of Systems. It provides near-real-time tactical and operational situational awareness by generating the \ac{cop} and \ac{ctpic}. GCCS-M processes and fuses tracks from ground, air, and maritime sensors, allowing commanders at \acp{moc} ashore and aboard carrier strike groups to synchronize decentralized execution across the battlespace.

Over time, PMW 150's mandate has been to evolve GCCS-M into a more agile, \ac{soa}. This evolution involves integrating \ac{mtc2} software applications, which dynamically plan, direct, monitor, and assess \ac{dmo} in real-time.

\textbf{Program 2: Naval Tactical Command Support System (NTCSS)}

\textbf{Status cue:} Legacy coursebook ACAT IAC; not confirmed on the current PMW~150 top-program sheet~\autocite{PEOC4I-PMW150-tearsheet-2026}.

While GCCS-M commands the tactical fight, NTCSS commands the logistics required to sustain it. In legacy coursebook framing, NTCSS was treated as a Mission Essential \ac{mais} (\ac{acat} IAC), a comprehensive suite of software applications providing shipboard and aviation maintenance management, inventory control, and financial business management. Established in 1995 through the merger of legacy systems (SNAP, NALCOMIS, and MRMS), NTCSS is installed on vessels and ashore to support the unit commanding officer in tracking repair work packages, aviation repairables, and critical parts inventory.

The sustainment of the legacy NTCSS ACAT           requires continuous configuration management and integration support. As PMW 150 shifts its focus toward modernized logistics frameworks, such as the \ac{nmro} and \ac{noss} initiatives, maintaining the stability of the NTCSS backbone is paramount to prevent operational degradation and supply chain failures during the transition period.

\subsubsection{PMW 160: Tactical Networks}
\label{ssubsec:pmw160}

\textbf{Program:} \ac{canes}

\textbf{ACAT Status:} ACAT IAC~\autocite{PEOC4I-PMW160-tearsheet-2025}

PMW 160 is tasked with providing mission-effective and cyber-resilient tactical networks to forces afloat. Its premier ACAT I program is \ac{canes}, a Chief of Naval Operations-directed initiative that serves as the indispensable core of the Navy's Information Warfare Platform.

\ac{canes} was initiated to collapse and replace five disparate legacy network systems into a single, consolidated afloat computing environment. Current PMW~160 public material groups the predecessor set as \ac{lns}: \ac{isns}, Sensitive Compartmented Information networks, CENTRIXS-M, and SubLAN~\autocite{PEOC4I-PMW160-tearsheet-2025}. It provides the necessary data transport, edge computing, voice, video, systems management, enterprise services, and cybersecurity functionality across multiple security domains for the fleet. Achieving a Full Deployment Decision (FDD) in October 2015, \ac{canes} operates under ASN (RD\&A) oversight as an ACAT IAC program.

\textbf{Related program:} \ac{adns} is PMW~160's ACAT~II tactical wide area network capability. It supplies surface ship, submarine, airborne, tactical-shore, and shore-based WAN gateway services and is the board cue for ship-to-shore IP routing~\autocite{PEOC4I-PMW160-tearsheet-2025}. In an afloat email example, the clean flow is: sailor application or workstation, \ac{canes} shipboard LAN/compute services, \ac{adns} routing and traffic engineering, SATCOM/RF bearer, shore gateway/Fleet Network Operations Center/DISN path, then the destination shore enterprise service.

\textbf{Strategic Insights:} CANES represents arguably the most foundational enabler of maritime combat power within NAVWAR. Without the hardware and software infrastructure provided by \ac{canes}, advanced analytical applications developed by PMW 120 (\ac{dcgsn}) and PMW 150 (GCCS-M) simply cannot function afloat. Recognizing the pace of cyber threats, PMW 160 is aggressively driving \ac{canes} toward a DevSecOps framework, decoupling software development from hardware procurement and ensuring that commercial-off-the-shelf (COTS) baselines are continuously evolved and patched through cyclical Service Pack updates.

\subsubsection{PMW/A 170: Communications and GPS Navigation}
\label{ssubsec:pmw170}

\textbf{Program:} Navy Multiband Terminal (NMT)

\textbf{ACAT Status:} ACAT IC~\autocite{PEOC4I-PMW170-tearsheet-2025}

PMW/A~170 serves as the premier provider of resilient communications and assured Position, Navigation, and Timing (PNT). The office's flagship ACAT I program is the \ac{nmt}.

\ac{nmt} is the Navy's sole protected satellite communications terminal, providing critical, secure, survivable, and interoperable \ac{milsatcom}. NMT enables high-capacity communication by providing simultaneous access to a variety of existing and future satellite constellations, including \ac{aehf}, \ac{wgs}, MILSTAR, \ac{dscs}, and the \ac{eps}.

PMW/A~170 is also cross-domain, but current PEO C4I and \ac{navair} sources style it as PMW/A~170 or PMW/A-170, not PMA/PMW~170. Use the published office designator rather than inferring a convention from PMA/PMW~101\autocite{PEOC4I-PMW170-tearsheet-2025,NAVAIR-PMWA170}.

\textbf{Strategic Insights:} In an era of great power competition, the electromagnetic spectrum is a highly contested domain. NMT is designed specifically to operate in \ac{a2ad} environments, featuring extreme anti-jam, anti-scintillation, and \ac{lpi}/\ac{lpd} capabilities using \ac{xdr} waveforms. The strategic importance of this ACAT IC program cannot be overstated; it supports the President's Ballistic Missile Defense priorities and the National Command Authority by ensuring the uninterrupted flow of critical strategic data, even under sustained electronic and physical attack.

\subsubsection{PEO Digital: Naval Enterprise Networks}
\label{ssubsec:pmw205}

\textbf{Portfolio:} \ac{nen}, including \ac{nmci}, ONE-Net, SMIT, and enterprise user services.

\textbf{Status cue:} Current public framing is enterprise network service/portfolio; do not teach \ac{ngen}/\ac{ngenr} ACAT I/MAIS as current unless separately sourced~\autocite{PEODigital-portfolio-overview-2023}.

Operating under the \ac{peodigital}, the Naval Enterprise Networks portfolio is responsible for enterprise-wide networks that provide secure, seamless, and global computer connectivity for the Department of the Navy ashore.

Legacy courseware often used \ac{ngen} and \ac{ngenr} as the acquisition shorthand for the follow-on to \ac{nmci}. Current PEO Digital public material emphasizes the broader \ac{nen} portfolio, including \ac{nmci}, ONE-Net, SMIT, and end-user hardware services, rather than presenting NGEN-R as a current ACAT I/MAIS program.

\textbf{Strategic Insights:} The board-level point is not the legacy label; it is that ashore enterprise networks are acquisition-critical infrastructure. The seamless operation of \ac{nen} services is a prerequisite for shore-based administrative, logistical, and acquisition planning operations.

\subsubsection{PMW 790: Shore and Expeditionary Integration}
\label{ssubsec:pmw790}

\textbf{Program:} \ac{djc2}

\textbf{ACAT Status:} ACAT IAC~\autocite{PEOC4I-PMW790-tearsheet-2025}

PMW 790 delivers resilient, interoperable shore and expeditionary C4I capabilities, ensuring that the infrastructure ashore matches the capacity of deployed fleets. The office's primary ACAT I capability is the \ac{djc2} system.

Originating as a Secretary of Defense priority transformation initiative, \ac{djc2} provides a standardized, self-contained, rapidly deployable, and scalable joint command and control infrastructure to Geographic Combatant Commands (GCCs) and Combined/Joint Task Force (C/JTF) Commanders. By integrating computer-network-enabled C2 applications with self-powered tactical shelters, \ac{djc2} allows commanders to establish a fully functional operations center in austere expeditionary environments rapidly. PMW 790 integrates this capability with over 60 unique shore and expeditionary networks, including the Shore Tactical Assured C2 (STACC) enterprise, to maintain global reach.

\textbf{Board cue:} The PMW~790 ACAT~I answer is \ac{djc2}, not D2GCS or DG2S.

\subsubsection{PEO MLB: Manpower, Logistics and Business Solutions}
\label{ssubsec:peomlb}

\textbf{Program:} Navy Enterprise Resource Planning (Navy ERP)

\textbf{ACAT Status:} BCAT I~\autocite{DAU-MDAP-Changes-2024,PEOMLB-portfolio-book-2025}

While strictly categorized as a BCAT~I defense business system under the \ac{bcac}, Navy ERP represents an enterprise IT investment equivalent in scale, complexity, and financial footprint to a traditional kinetic \ac{acat} I weapon system. DAU's 2024 MDAP change notice associates Navy ERP Financial IT Services with PMW~220, and current \ac{peomlb} public materials describe Navy ERP+ modernization following a June~2024 ASN(RD\&A) acquisition decision memorandum. The board cue is that Navy ERP standardizes Department of the Navy business processes for acquisition, financial management, and logistics operations.

Current PEO MLB public material organizes the office around capability portfolios rather than the PMW~120/150/160-style construct used by PEO C4I. The board-safe portfolio list is MyNavy HR IT Solutions, Ready Relevant Learning, Navy ERP Financial IT Services, Logistics IT Services, Marine Corps Logistics Integrated Information Solutions, Naval Applications and Business Services, and Marine Corps Manpower IT Systems Modernization~\autocite{PEOMLB-portfolio-book-2025}.

Combining commercial off-the-shelf software (primarily SAP architecture) with extensive business process reengineering, Navy ERP utilizes a single database to manage shared common data across functional communities. As of recent reporting, Navy ERP controls over 50 percent of the Navy's financial spending, manages global supply operations, and provides workforce management for over 65,000 personnel. By modernizing its code and upgrading software capabilities, Navy ERP serves as the fundamental enabler for the DoD's strategic goal of reforming business practices for greater performance and affordability, providing the auditability and financial tracking required for every other ACAT I program in the Navy's portfolio.

\subsection{Coursebook cue: Fleet Readiness Directorate}

For NAVWAR organization questions, distinguish program offices from the Fleet
Readiness Directorate (FRD).  FRD is NAVWAR's fleet-facing readiness integrator:
it is the fleet single point of entry, NAVWAR single point of accountability,
and the organization that drives execution rigor across the enterprise for
fleet support and installations.  Coursebook 2.1.3 identifies the two core
branches as FRD 100 Fleet Support and FRD 200 Fleet Installations
\autocite{edo-2-1-3-navwar-enterprise-2024}.

\paragraph{Board cue.}
FRD is the fleet-facing accountability and execution-rigor arm, not a PEO.  Name
FRD 100 Fleet Support and FRD 200 Fleet Installations if asked for the branches.
