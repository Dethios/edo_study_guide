\section{Manufacturing and Producibility}

\subsection{Summary}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Acquisition issue.}] Manufacturing is a cost, schedule, performance, quality, and industrial-base risk that must be shaped while the design is still tradeable~\autocite{edo-3-7-1-manufacturing-producibility-2025,DoD-Producibility-Manufacturability-Guide-2024}.
  \item[\textbf{Producibility.}] Producibility and manufacturability improve ease of manufacture, repeatability, product quality, reliability, maintainability, and life-cycle affordability~\autocite{DoD-Producibility-Manufacturability-Guide-2024}.
  \item[\textbf{Readiness evidence.}] Use \acp{mrl} and \ac{mra} evidence to expose maturity gaps in design, materials, process capability, supply base, facilities, tooling, workforce, quality, and rate assumptions before production decisions~\autocite{DoD-MRL-Deskbook-2025,DoD-Producibility-Manufacturability-Guide-2024}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Why manufacturing belongs in acquisition decisions}

Manufacturing is not a post-design activity.  A program can meet technical
requirements on paper and still fail because the design cannot be produced,
tested, funded, or sustained at the required rate.  Coursebook 3.7.1 frames the
manufacturing decision as a cost, schedule, and performance control problem:
the program manager must use producibility thinking early enough to influence
system design, supplier choices, manufacturing planning, and quality management
before production commitments become expensive to reverse
\autocite{edo-3-7-1-manufacturing-producibility-2025,DoD-Producibility-Manufacturability-Guide-2024}.

\subsection{Manufacturing objectives for design}

The manufacturing objective is to make the design producible, affordable, and
testable without losing the operational capability that justified the program.
The board answer should connect three levers
\autocite{edo-3-7-1-manufacturing-producibility-2025,DoD-Producibility-Manufacturability-Guide-2024}:

\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Design.}] Develop a producible and testable design rather than a design that only works in engineering demonstration.
  \item[\textbf{Process.}] Design and mature manufacturing processes that can reliably build the item.
  \item[\textbf{Process control.}] Establish process controls that satisfy requirements while minimizing avoidable cost.
\end{description}

\subsection{Manufacturing Readiness Board Cue}

Use \ac{mra}/\acp{mrl} to ask whether the design, materials, suppliers, tooling,
process controls, workforce, facilities, quality system, and cost/rate assumptions
are mature enough for the next production decision.  Design for manufacturing
and assembly (DFM/A) is the design-side
lever: simplify parts and assembly, control tolerances, reduce special processes,
and design for repeatable build/test flow
\autocite{DoD-MRL-Deskbook-2025,DoD-Producibility-Manufacturability-Guide-2024,DAU-Manufacturing-Quality}.

\subsection{Producibility trade space}

Producibility is a trade, not a slogan.  The program manager should be ready to
explain how operational performance, reliability, availability, supportability,
technology constraints, ownership cost, schedule, and producibility interact.
A design that maximizes one attribute can damage another; for example, a very
tight tolerance may improve a performance margin while increasing supplier
yield loss and delaying delivery
\autocite{edo-3-7-1-manufacturing-producibility-2025,DoD-Producibility-Manufacturability-Guide-2024}.

\paragraph{Board cue.}
If asked why an EDO cares about manufacturing, answer: production is where
design, funding, industrial base, quality, and schedule converge.  Producibility
must be designed in before production locks in cost and risk.

\subsection{Quick Review}
\begin{itemize}
  \item What is the board trap in manufacturing? \textbf{Answer:} Treating manufacturing as post-design execution instead of an early acquisition-risk and design-trade issue.
  \item What do \ac{mra}/\acp{mrl} add? \textbf{Answer:} Evidence of manufacturing maturity gaps, risk, and maturation work needed before production or rate decisions.
  \item Why does design for manufacturing and assembly matter? \textbf{Answer:} It simplifies build and assembly while requirements are still tradeable, reducing later yield, quality, schedule, and cost risk.
\end{itemize}
