\section{Program Funding and Execution}

\subsection{Program Funding}
\subsubsection{Financial terms}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Budget authority.}] \ac{ba} is authority granted by appropriation law to enter obligations that will result in immediate or future outlays~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Commitment.}] Administrative reservation of funds (usually by the local comptroller) in anticipation of a future obligation~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Obligation.}] Legal reservation of funds to make a future payment of money~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Expenditure.}] Charge against available funds resulting from a voucher, claim, or other document approved by competent authority~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Outlay.}] Occurs when the vendor cashes the expenditure check and money flows from the Treasury to the vendor or supplier~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
\end{description}

\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Credit-card analogy.}] \ac{ba} is the line of credit, commitment is the intent to purchase, obligation is the charge slip, expenditure is the bill/check, and outlay is the cash transfer from Treasury~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Appropriation life-cycle and expired funds}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Current.}] Funds are available for new obligations during the appropriation's obligation window (1--5 years depending on category)~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Expired.}] A 5-year period where funds are no longer available for new obligations but may be used for obligation adjustments, expenditures, and outlays~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Canceled.}] Funds are closed; remaining balances return to Treasury~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
\end{description}

\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Expired funds example 1.}] In Aug 2025, FY22 \ac{opn} funds are expired (FY22--FY24 obligation window), so a \ac{pco} cannot sign a new contract; the funds are limited to obligation adjustments, expenditures, and outlays~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Expired funds example 2.}] In Jan 2025, FY24 \ac{rdte} funds are still current (FY24--FY25), so a \ac{pco} may sign a new contract for work starting in FY25~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
\end{description}

\note{Obligation windows: \ac{milpers} and \ac{omn} are 1 year; \ac{rdte} is 2 years; procurement is 3 years (\ac{scn} is 5); \ac{milcon} is 5 years~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.}

\subsubsection{Funding policies}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Annual funding.}] Applies to expense appropriations (\ac{omn}, \ac{milpers}); request \ac{ba} only for the goods or services needed in a given fiscal year, with a major exception for non-severable service contracts that cross fiscal years~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Incremental funding.}] Applies to \ac{rdte}; request funding only for work expected to be performed in the given fiscal year. The Navy is authorized to use incremental funding for \ac{scn} on certain ship programs (\ac{cvn}, \ac{ssbn}, \ac{lha}/\ac{lhd})~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Full funding.}] Applies to procurement and \ac{milcon}; annual budget requests must cover the total cost of complete, usable end items delivered within a 12-month period. If multiple end items are bought, do not budget for more items than can be delivered within 12 months; options can extend procurement across years~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
\end{description}

\note{Incremental funding example: A 28-month, \$260M \ac{rdte} effort with FY24--FY26 costs of 35\%/45\%/20\% would request \$91M (FY24), \$117M (FY25), and \$52M (FY26)~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.}

\note{Full funding practical: A \ac{wpn} contract with deliveries accepted over two years violates full funding; fix by structuring lots or options so each lot delivers within 12 months and is funded with the year-of-delivery appropriation~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.}

\subsubsection{Exceptions to full funding}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Advance procurement (long lead items).}] An exception to full funding for long-lead components; documented in the acquisition strategy, approved at milestone, and funded as a separate line item to protect schedules or critical skills~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
\end{description}

\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Multiyear procurement (MYP).}] A single contract for a specific quantity of usable end items delivered over more than one year (no more than five). Congress authorizes the full contract scope at award (NDAA), but funding and appropriations remain annual~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Benefits.}] Stability in acquisition and budgeting, productivity gains, cost growth reduction, \ac{eoq} opportunities, and reluctance to cancel once in place~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Challenges.}] Termination liability if funding is not provided, inability to budget for termination liability, and political controversy (Congress and \ac{osd})~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Required criteria.}] Savings relative to annual contracting (guidance ~10\%), stable requirements/design, confidence in cost estimates and production capability, and commitment to funding by Service, \ac{osd}, and Congress~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Initiation.}] Proposed in the President's Budget; if approved, authorized by the NDAA and funded through annual appropriations on a year-by-year basis~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
\end{description}

\note{DDG-51 \ac{myp} example: The FY98--FY01 and FY02--FY05 \ac{myp} contracts saved over \$1B; the FY13--FY17 \ac{myp} saved about \$1.5B and enabled a 10th ship option~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.}

\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Other exceptions.}] \ac{bb} (fewer restrictions than \ac{myp}), cost-to-complete (prior-year completion), and incremental funding for \ac{rcoh}~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Funding product improvements}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Phase 1: Development and testing.}] \ac{rdte} when the modification enhances performance or requires independent \ac{dt}/\ac{iote}; otherwise Procurement for in-production systems, or \ac{omn} if the system is no longer in production~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Phase 2: Fabrication.}] Procurement~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Phase 3: Installation.}] Procurement~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsubsection{MILCON funding}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Program.}] The acquisition budget culminates in the annual Military Construction Program submitted to Congress for authorization and appropriation~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Threshold.}] \ac{milcon} is the acquisition (design and/or construction) of facilities costing more than \$4,000,000 in support of active and reserve requirements~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Sustainment.}] Maintenance and repair activities funded with \ac{omn}~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Modernization.}] Alteration or replacement of facilities to implement new standards, new functions, or components with 50+ year service life; funded with OPN~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Rule.}] It is expressly forbidden to incrementally fund a project to avoid \ac{milcon} triggers~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Summary check}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Policies.}] Annual (\ac{omn}, \ac{milpers}); Incremental (\ac{rdte}, select \ac{scn}); Full Funding (Procurement, \ac{milcon})~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Full funding exceptions.}] Advance Procurement and \ac{myp} are the primary exceptions; \ac{bb}, cost-to-complete, and \ac{rcoh} are additional cases~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Product improvements.}] Phase 1 uses \ac{rdte}/Procurement/\ac{omn} depending on testing and production status; Phases 2--3 use Procurement~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Expired funds rule.}] Expired funds cannot create new obligations; they are limited to obligation adjustments, expenditures, and outlays~\autocite{edo-3-1-3-program-funding-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Budget Execution}
\subsubsection{Execution terminology and flow}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Budget authority.}] Authority granted by appropriation law to enter obligations that will result in immediate or future outlays~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Commitment.}] Administrative reservation of funds (usually by the local comptroller) in anticipation of a future obligation~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Obligation.}] Legal reservation of funds to make a future payment of money~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Expenditure.}] Charge against available funds resulting from a voucher, claim, or other document approved by competent authority~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Outlay.}] Occurs when the vendor cashes the expenditure check and money flows from Treasury to the vendor or supplier~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Reprogramming.}] Redirecting funds for purposes other than those contemplated at the time of appropriation; rules are based on agreements between \ac{dod} and Congress, and \ac{dod} reports reprogramming semi-annually to Congress~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Obligation plan.}] Month-by-month projection of available funds for new obligations for each fiscal year; prepared by the \ac{pmo}~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Expenditure plan.}] Month-by-month projection of expenditures for each fiscal year of funding; required for all appropriation years not fully expended, even after the obligation window closes~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
\end{description}

\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Execution flow.}] \ac{pmo} submits a purchase request, the comptroller certifies funds (commitment), the contracting officer signs the contract (obligation), the finance office issues payment (expenditure), and the Treasury disburses cash (outlay)~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Execution laws (purpose, amount, time)}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Misappropriation Act (Purpose).}] Funds must be used only for the purposes and programs for which the appropriation was made (31 U.S.C. 1301(a))~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Anti-Deficiency Act (Amount).}] Prohibits obligations in excess of amounts available or apportioned; forbids obligations in advance of appropriations and requires accountability for violations (31 U.S.C. 1341 and 1517)~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Bona Fide Need Rule (Time).}] Funds must meet a bona fide need in the year of the appropriation's obligation window (31 U.S.C. 1502)~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
\end{description}

\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Which law?}] Obligating funds before an appropriation act violates the Anti-Deficiency Act; using Procurement funds for an \ac{rdte} shortfall violates the Misappropriation Act; obligating FY04 \ac{rdte} for FY08 services violates the Bona Fide Need Rule~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Spend plans (obligation and expenditure)}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Required for.}] Procurement line items, \ac{rdte} program elements, and \ac{omn} sub-activity groups each require spend plans~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Obligation plan.}] Month-by-month projection of new obligations; prepared by the \ac{pmo}~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Expenditure plan.}] Month-by-month projection of expenditures; maintained for all appropriation years not fully expended~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Basis.}] Spend plans are essential tools for managing execution and are based on the \ac{bes}~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
\end{description}

\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Failure to execute.}] Programs that miss obligation or expenditure goals become targets for reprogramming, risk losing funding, and face future budget adjustments~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsubsection{Reprogramming types}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Congressional prior approval (PA).}] Required for congressional special interest items, major end item quantity increases, use of transfer authority, actions above \ac{btr} thresholds, and program starts/cancellations. Threshold guidance is \$15M for \ac{milpers}, \ac{omn}, Procurement, and \ac{rdte}. \ac{secdef} requests approval from HAC/SAC/HASC/SASC and, when applicable, intelligence committees~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Congressional notification.}] Certain actions require notification to Congress even if they do not need prior approval~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Internal reprogramming (IR).}] Creates an audit trail for actions not requiring congressional approval; used to reclassify funds between line items or program elements without changing program substance and to execute transfer authority accounts~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Below-threshold reprogramming (BTR).}] Component-level reprogramming below congressional thresholds; controlled by comptrollers and generally does not require congressional involvement. \ac{btr} cannot change appropriation categories or execute actions requiring congressional approval~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Letter transfer.}] Used to process congressionally directed transfers (e.g., Defense Health Program to Department of Veterans Affairs)~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Level of control.}] Transfers below the appropriation's level of control are not considered reprogramming and can be approved by the program manager~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
\end{description}

\note{Congress recognizes the need for flexibility in execution, but reprogramming rules are based on \ac{dod}/Congress agreements and reported to Congress semi-annually~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.}

\note{Example (PA): Virginia-class submarine reprogramming realigned \$29.8M of SCN 04/08 and transferred \$10.2M (OPN 04/06) and \$9.0M (\ac{rdte} 04/05) to SCN~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.}

\subsubsection{Funding cuts and impact statements}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Impact statement.}] A written assessment of the effects a budget cut will have on a program~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Rules.}] Provide specific, credible impacts; use simple language and spell out acronyms; be prepared to follow through with program changes cited~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Contents.}] Operational impact (capability and delay), business case (cost growth), policy non-compliance risks, and program impact tied to operational impact and \ac{apb} deviations~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
\end{description}

\note{Example rewrite: A vague staffing loss becomes a quantified operational impact (e.g., satellite launch delayed two months, reducing coverage to 65\%)~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.}

\subsubsection{Funding cuts: impacts on industry}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Proposals.}] Bidders may need to revise price, performance, or schedule~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Workforce impacts.}] Contractors may shift resources and personnel; overhead rates can rise as workload declines~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Cost case.}] Will-cost/should-cost analysis helps justify the current budget by showing savings already identified and implemented~\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Coursebook cue: apportionment, allocation, and allotment}

Budget execution authority moves in controlled steps.  An appropriation gives
Congressional authority; an apportionment limits how the executive branch may
use that authority over time, program, or activity; an allocation distributes
authority to subordinate organizations; and an allotment is the formal
authorization that permits an accountable activity to incur obligations
\autocite{edo-3-1-4-budget-execution-2025}.

\begin{longtblr}[
  caption = {Budget execution authority chain.},
  label = {tab:budget-execution-authority-chain},
]{
  width = \linewidth,
  colspec = {X[0.7,l] X[1.7,l]},
  rowhead = 1,
  hlines,
  vlines,
}
\textbf{Step} & \textbf{Board cue} \\
Appropriation & Congress provides budget authority for a purpose, time, and amount. \\
Apportionment & OMB controls the rate or category of obligation to prevent overexecution. \\
Allocation & A higher headquarters distributes authority to a subordinate command or account. \\
Allotment & The accountable activity receives authority to incur obligations within the stated limits. \\
\end{longtblr}

\paragraph{Board cue.}
Anti-deficiency risk is measured against the legally controlling limit, not just
the headline appropriation total.
