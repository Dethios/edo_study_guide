\section{Fleet Maintenance Policy and Implementation}

\subsection{Summary}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Policy baseline.}] \ac{opnavinst} 4700.7 sets ship maintenance policy and requires \ac{rcm} for new ships, systems, and equipment~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Maintenance strategies.}] \ac{rcm} underpins \ac{cbm} and \ac{tdm} strategies; the Navy uses \ac{cbm} to the maximum extent available~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Key documents.}] \ac{cmp}, \ac{icmp}, \ac{opnavnote} 4700, and \ac{ndenm} define requirements, intervals, and schedules for depot maintenance~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Scheduling governance.}] \ac{fast} integrates operational requirements with \ac{opnavnote} 4700 and recommends schedules to the \ac{fmbod} for approval~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Availability types.}] \ac{cno} scheduled and Fleet scheduled availabilities, plus class-specific maintenance strategies, structure depot maintenance execution across the fleet~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Fleet Maintenance Policy}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Readiness and service life.}] Ships are maintained to the highest practical readiness, to meet required operational availability, in a safe material condition, to meet habitability standards, and to achieve expected service life~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Right level of maintenance.}] Work is performed by the maintenance level best suited to accomplish the task, balancing law, urgency, capability, capacity, crew impact, and total cost~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{RCM requirement.}] New ships and systems use \ac{rcm} methodology to define maintenance requirements~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Influences on Maintenance Policy}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Force structure: size of fleet and mix of ship classes~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item Operational transformation: surge and pulse demands, minimal manning approaches, and increased forward presence~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item \ac{ofrp} alignment, industrial capacity, and the balance of public and private sector capability~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item Proprietary and \ac{oem} information, available funding, and readiness drivers such as continuous maintenance and \ac{cbm}~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Maintenance Principles}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{RCM.}] An engineering discipline that determines maintenance requirements based on likely functional failures and their impact on safety, operations, and life-cycle cost~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{CBM.}] Uses objective evidence of need (e.g., sensors, inspections, diagnostics) to schedule maintenance and sustain safety, reliability, availability, and cost targets~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{TDM.}] Time-directed tasks restore resistance to failure on a fixed interval regardless of condition when no effective condition-directed task exists~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Failure-finding tasks.}] Tests identify hidden failures in protective devices, such as smoke detectors, to ensure readiness~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Task effectiveness.}] Tasks must be applicable and effective to preserve inherent reliability at minimum cost~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Maintenance Philosophy}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \ac{rcm} is the core maintenance philosophy; \ac{cbm} and \ac{tdm} are the primary strategies derived from its analysis~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item Navy maintenance policy applies \ac{cbm} to the maximum extent available using \ac{rcm} rules~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Depot Maintenance Framework}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{OPNAVINST 4700.7.}] Establishes overall policy, broad O/I/D maintenance concepts, \ac{cmp} requirements, and organizational responsibilities~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{CMP.}] Defines maintenance strategy, availability cycle, and tasks by scope, level, and periodicity; developed by the \ac{spm} and technical community based on \ac{tfp} guidance~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{TFP.}] Captures notional class maintenance requirements across expected service life and informs programming and budgeting~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Ship sheets.}] Detail maintenance requirements by hull across the \ac{fydp} and support the annual \ac{pom} process~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{ICMP.}] Transitions class plans to a CBM-based, equipment-oriented approach with standard tasks and consistent planning data~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{OPNAVNOTE 4700.}] Consolidates class maintenance schedules and publishes notional intervals, durations, and man-days by ship class~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{NDE-NM.}] Provides the current and future schedule of depot maintenance by hull and balances operational schedules with industrial workload~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{CNO Availability Scheduling and Approval}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{FAST.}] Integrates \ac{opnavnote} 4700 with operational requirements and proposes workload allocations by shipyard capacity, homeport, and private sector availability for \ac{fmbod} approval~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{FAST membership.}] Includes \ac{opnav} N83, N2/N6, N95, N96, fleet N43 representatives, \ac{cnrmc}/SEA 04, \ac{tycom}s, \ac{navwar}, and other \acp{pmo} as required~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{FMBOD.}] Fleet Maintenance Officers chair the board with \ac{navsea} 04/08, \ac{opnav} N43, \ac{tycom} N43, and \ac{cnrmc}/SEA 21 membership~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Scheduling artifacts.}] \ac{warr} products from naval shipyards and port loading charts from \acp{rmc} inform workload and resource decisions~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Public-Private Split and Assignment Considerations}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{50/50 rule.}] Title 10 U.S.C. Section 2466 requires at least 50 percent of \ac{dod} depot maintenance funding to be executed in public depots; no more than 50 percent may go to private industry~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Exclusions.}] \ac{cvn} refueling and contracted elements of public shipyard work packages are excluded from the 50/50 calculation~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{NSY assignments.}] Nuclear ship work, urgent operational commitments, homeport skills, workload capacity, and drydock availability drive public shipyard assignments~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Private competition.}] Conventional surface ships and large deck dry-docking are competed with homeport or coast-wide bids based on availability length and contractor capacity~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Budget and Special Cases}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Availabilities often have multiple customers; each contributes maintenance and modernization funding as appropriate~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item \ac{fydp} maintenance estimates include notional work and emergent growth, while modernization estimates are based on planned alterations~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item President's Budget updates refine estimates as execution dates approach~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item Urgent repair, emergent dry docking, and collision or battle damage follow the same approval process, with stakeholders informed on scheduling impacts~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Types of Availabilities}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{CNO scheduled.}] \ac{roh}, \ac{coh}, \ac{ero}, \ac{pma}, \ac{dmp}, \ac{pia}, \ac{dpia}, \ac{sra}, \ac{dsra}, and \ac{cia} are among the scheduled depot availability categories~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Fleet scheduled.}] \ac{tav}, \ac{rav}, \ac{fmav}, \ac{cmav}, and \ac{vr} are Fleet-scheduled availability types~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Maintenance Strategies}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{EOC.}] A structured approach to sustain or increase operational availability through periodic inspections, planned maintenance, scheduled availabilities, and modernization; submarine examples include \ac{dmp} at 120 months and \ac{eoh} at 240 months for 688-class boats~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{PROG.}] Supports reduced manning ship classes with limited organizational maintenance, forward deployment, major modernization every 10 years, a rotatable pool, and a dedicated repair facility~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Phased maintenance.}] Depot work is executed through short, frequent \acp{pma}, with repairs authorized by port engineers or maintenance managers and performed in homeport~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{IMP.}] Keeps \ac{cvn} 68 and 78 classes in acceptable material condition using \acp{cia}, \acp{pia}, and \acp{dpia}, with mid-life \ac{rcoh} still required~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Continuous maintenance.}] Depot-level work flows continuously outside scheduled \ac{cno} availabilities, shifting to a decentralized, condition-based process that validates \ac{csmp} and maintenance cost knowledge in real time~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Optimized Fleet Response Plan}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{O-FRP objectives.}] \ac{ofrp} aligns maintenance, training, evaluations, and a single eight-month deployment within a 36-month cycle to increase readiness and predictability~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Availability alignment.}] O-FRP integrates maintenance and modernization planning for carrier strike groups, amphibious ready groups, and submarines, supporting surge readiness and port loading adjustments~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Modernization integration.}] Modernization improvements are synchronized with integrated maintenance, including \ac{sovt} and supporting system verification~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Quick Review}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Overall ship maintenance policy is defined by \ac{opnavinst} 4700.7~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item \ac{cmp} defines maintenance policy for a ship class and is developed by the \ac{spm}~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item \ac{prog} is the maintenance philosophy centered on major modernization every 10 years and dedicated repair facilities~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
  \item \ac{imp} applies to \ac{cvn} 68 and 78 classes~\autocite{edo-4-1-3-fleet-maint-policy-2025}.
\end{itemize}
