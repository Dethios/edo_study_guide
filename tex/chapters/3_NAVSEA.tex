\section{NAVSEA Organization and Warfare Centers}

\subsection{Summary}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \ac{navsea} is the Navy systems command for ships, submarines, combat systems, ship systems, technical authority, acquisition support, maintenance, modernization, demilitarization, and disposal.
  \item For board answers, lead with mission and functions before live headcount or budget numbers. NAVSEA public pages use different page-specific snapshots, so exact counts are source-dependent and should be checked at board-prep time.
  \item Know the directorate cues: SEA~04 is industrial operations, shipyards, logistics, and SUPSHIP execution; SEA~05 is naval systems engineering and \ac{ta}; SEA~07/Team Submarines is undersea life-cycle support; SEA~21 is in-service surface maintenance and modernization.
  \item Treat the 2026 \ac{pae} restructuring as acquisition-governance realignment. It changes portfolio accountability, but it does not erase NAVSEA technical authority, PEO/IWS program-office recall, or warfare-center technical depth.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Mission, priorities, and vision}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Mission.}] Design, build, deliver, and maintain ships, submarines, and systems reliably, on time, and on cost for the United States Navy. \ac{navsea} is the force behind the fleet, with the primary mission of life-cycle engineering of ships and ship systems~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Mission priorities.}] Deliver combat power, transform digital capabilities, and build a team to compete and win~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Vision.}] ``Expand the advantage'' by ensuring the United States Navy can protect and defend America, operating with a modern industrial base, serving as a world-class employer of choice, and setting the value-added standard for acquisition, engineering, business, and maintenance~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Major functions and scope}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Major functions.}] Oversee core processes for acquisition, in-service support, weapon systems, \ac{it}, engineering systems, cybersecurity systems, demilitarization, and disposal. Core functions include cost estimating, technical development and readiness assessment, systems engineering, manufacturing, integrated logistics, maintenance and modernization, and comptroller/legal/contracting support services~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Assigned ships and systems.}] Surface ships, aircraft carriers, submarines, submersibles, \ac{hme} systems, combat systems, ordnance, chemical/biological/radiological defense material, and towing/salvage equipage~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Technical authority.}] Exercises technical and certification authority across ships and ship systems~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Corporate overview}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Organization.}] Headquarters command staff and directorates at Washington Navy Yard provide policy, guidance, oversight, and support across financial management, contracting, logistics/maintenance/industrial operations, engineering, undersea warfare, corporate operations, \ac{it}, legal support, and security~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025,NAVSEA-Directorates-2026}.
  \item[\textbf{Current public snapshots.}] NAVSEA public pages are not a single synchronized live ledger. The Directorates page describes more than 85{,}299 civilian and military personnel in 42 activities as of 1~Aug~2022, while the Warfare Centers ``Who We Are'' page describes the largest Navy \ac{syscom}, nearly one-fifth of the Navy budget, more than 150 acquisition programs, and more than 75{,}000 civilian and military employees at 33 activities in 16 states~\autocite{NAVSEA-Directorates-2026,NAVSEA-Warfare-Centers-WhoWeAre-2026}.
  \item[\textbf{Board cue.}] If asked how big NAVSEA is, answer with the functional scope first: ships, submarines, combat systems, technical authority, acquisition support, sustainment, shipyards, RMCs, SUPSHIPs, and warfare centers. Quote exact manpower, activity, program-count, or budget figures only with the date-stamped public page used for that answer.
\end{description}

\subsection{Organizational management approaches}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{Functional organization.}] Expertise grouped by discipline (e.g., engineering, contracting, logistics), emphasizing depth and standardization~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Product/project organization.}] Resources organized around a specific platform or product line to drive ownership, schedule focus, and rapid decisions~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{Matrix organization.}] Functional resources support projects as needed; \ac{navsea} and affiliated \acp{peo} operate as a matrix to balance technical authority with program execution~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Program management relationships}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{PEO reporting.}] \acp{peo} report to the \ac{asnrdanda} for acquisition matters and to \ac{comnavsea} for in-service support and modernization~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{SPM and PARM.}] The \ac{spm} owns platform acquisition and life-cycle management; the \ac{parm} manages acquisition and life-cycle support for major subsystems (e.g., combat systems)~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{SHAPM and SLM.}] \ac{shapm} focuses on acquisition for the ship/submarine platform; \ac{slm} manages in-service life-cycle responsibilities for the platform~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{SPD.}] The \ac{spd} is the contract-like agreement between the platform manager (\ac{spm} or \ac{shapm}/\ac{slm}) and the \ac{parm}; it may be fulfilled by a 7300 document~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Program Executive Offices (PEOs) affiliated with NAVSEA}
\begin{description}
  \item[\textbf{PEO (role).}] Directs multiple acquisition programs and major system portfolios; reports to \ac{asnrdanda} for acquisition matters and to \ac{comnavsea} for in-service support. \acp{pm} report to the \ac{peo} and \ac{mdauthority} for program execution~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{PEO Ships.}] Manages acquisition for surface combatants (destroyers and next-generation destroyer programs), amphibious ships (\ac{lhd}/\ac{lha}/\ac{lpd}), and auxiliary/support ships, plus related activities including \ac{fms} and the Electric Ships Office~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{PEO USC.}] Delivers unmanned maritime systems, mine warfare systems, and small surface combatants such as \ac{lcs} and the Constellation-class frigate, emphasizing rapid delivery to the fleet~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{PEO SSN.}] Focal point for attack submarine design, construction, and in-service support (e.g., Virginia-class new construction and in-service programs, advanced undersea systems, and \ac{submepp})~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{PEO SSBN.}] Manages Columbia-class ballistic missile submarine design, construction, and in-service support, plus Ohio-class service life extension~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{PEO UWS.}] Oversees submarine combat, weapons, acoustic, electromagnetic, surveillance, communications, training, and safety systems to improve undersea warfighting and cybersecurity across submarine platforms~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{PEO IWS.}] Responsible for surface ship combat systems (combat management, sensors, missiles, launchers, electronic warfare, gun systems), open-architecture integration, and field activities such as the Aegis Technical Representative and Surface Combat Systems Center~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
  \item[\textbf{PEO CVN.}] Manages carrier \ac{rdte}, design, construction, midlife refueling/overhauls, and life-cycle support, including PMS~312 (in-service), PMS~378 (CVN~78), and PMS~379 (CVN~79/80)~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025}.
\end{description}

\subsection{PEO IWS Program Offices}
\label{subsec:peoiws-programs}

\ac{peoiws} is tasked with developing, delivering, and sustaining operationally dominant integrated combat systems, encompassing surface radars, missiles, launchers, electronic warfare suites, undersea sensors, and integrated shipboard computing environments. PEO IWS leadership and billet incumbency should be confirmed from the current official NAVSEA PEO IWS page at board-prep time; if not verified from the live source, answer with office role and portfolio rather than a name~\autocite{navsea-peo-iws-homepage-2026}. The \ac{edo} coursebook and current NAVSEA public pages divide the numbered offices into Integrated Combat Systems (ICS) Major Program Managers and Product Line Major Program Managers. ICS offices plan, upgrade, integrate, and sustain surface combat-system baselines for ship or fleet groupings; Product Line offices develop and sustain combat-system components such as sensors, weapons, undersea systems, command and control, and terminal-defense systems~\autocite{edo-2-1-2-navsea-organization-2025,navsea-peo-iws-about-2026,navsea-peo-iws-industry-engagement-2026}.

\subsubsection{Key ACAT I Programs within PEO IWS}
\label{ssubsec:peoiws-acat-i}

\textbf{IWS 1.0 (AEGIS Combat Systems):} The \ac{amod} program ensures the combat system outpaces evolving threats via the \ac{acb} process. Through \ac{acb} methodology, IWS 1.0 continuously integrates commercial hardware refreshes---specifically the \ac{cps} and \ac{cds}---with software capability expansions. The DDG 51 Flight III upgrade program elevated the DDG 51 program to ACAT 1D status due to extreme cost and strategic impact.

\textbf{IWS 2.0 (Above Water Sensors):} The \ac{amdr}, designated AN/SPY-6, represents a generational leap over legacy AN/SPY-1. Utilizing AESA technology built upon scalable \acp{rma}, SPY-6 provides unprecedented sensitivity, range, and target discrimination. It is the mandatory sensor enabling Flight III DDG 51 class to execute \ac{iamd} against ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and advanced aircraft.

\textbf{IWS 3.0 (Surface Ship Weapons):} The SM-6 (Standard Missile 6) is an ACAT I major defense acquisition program. By integrating the SM-2 block airframe with the active radar seeker of the AIM-120 AMRAAM, SM-6 provides over-the-horizon air defense, anti-surface warfare (ASuW), and terminal-phase ballistic missile defense (BMD) capabilities. Its active seeker enables ``engage-on-remote'' (EoR) tactics that vastly expand strike group engagement envelopes.

\textbf{IWS 6.0 (Command and Control):} The Cooperative Engagement Capability (CEC) is an ACAT IC program that shares raw, real-time radar measurement data among surface ships and aircraft. This sensor netting creates a single, integrated, fire-control-quality composite track picture, transcending traditional tactical data links such as Link 16. CEC is the ultimate equalizer against radar horizon limitations~\autocite{navsea-cec-enhancing-battlefield-awareness-2025}.
